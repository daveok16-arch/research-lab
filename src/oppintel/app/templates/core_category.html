What this platform is

A commercial construction lead is a project worth investigating. Most lead lists assemble one from a database entry and a contact. This platform assembles one from the public records a city already publishes, keeps the evidence attached to every field, and states plainly where the record is silent.

The result is narrower than a scraped list and considerably more useful: fewer projects, each of which you can check against its source before your estimator spends an afternoon on it.

Who it is for

Commercial {{ trade.label | lower }} contractors and the teams around them — estimators, business development, and sales. The directory is scoped to the active trade's evidence, so a {{ trade.short_label }} view lists projects with mechanical activity rather than every building permit in the market.

How opportunities are discovered

Public permit records are collected from each participating city, normalised into a common shape, and clustered by address into the underlying project. A mechanical permit, or mechanical scope named in the work description, is what marks a project as relevant to this trade.

How matching and evidence work

Every project is classified against documented evidence, not against opinion. Fields a source does not publish stay empty and render as Not verified. When two publishers disagree about a value, both are kept and the field is flagged, rather than one being silently preferred.

Monitoring, alerts and organisation

Watching a project asks the platform to re-check it on every new collection, and to tell you when the record actually changes — a new permit, a status change, new mechanical evidence. Each alert names the change and the source behind it. You can save projects, keep private notes and tags, and move them through your own pipeline stages.

What this platform does not claim

A project appearing here is relevant to {{ trade.short_label | lower }} work because a public record shows mechanical activity. It is not a confirmed open bid. No source in this market publishes bid status, so the platform never states one. The pipeline stages are your own filing decisions, not a statement about a project's procurement.

Coverage is not complete for every jurisdiction in the market, and some source record types are capped by a pagination limit, so their totals are lower bounds.
